90% interval 2.2% to 9.1% - share of 75 comparable DefiLlama protocols that grew fees enough - snapshot t0 = 2026-11-12 - verdict: buybacks are unlikely to cover issuance at the 24-month mark.

Method: buybacks cover 18.5% of net issuance today, so NEAR Intents fees must grow 5.67x in 24 months; the number above is how often protocols of the same size and age did that.

  • The question: NEAR spends part of the NEAR Intents fees on buying back NEAR. In 24 months from now, will these buybacks absorb all the new NEAR that the protocol issues?
  • Today: they absorb 18.5% of it (dollar value, last 90 days to 2026-11-12).
  • The bar: to absorb all of it, NEAR Intents fees must grow 5.67x in 24 months, from $77.5M to $439.2M a year, with price, issuance and the share of fees spent on buybacks unchanged.
  • The odds: 5.3%, about 1 chance in 19: the share of 75 DeFi protocols that once had NEAR Intents' size and age and then grew that much in 24 months. Replayed on 201 past 24-month forecasts, this method was too optimistic (fee doubling forecast 25.3%, seen 3.1%), so the real odds are more likely lower than higher.
  • For an investor: On base rates the buyback does not offset dilution by the 24-month mark: net supply growth stays the base case. A case for NEAR should not rest on buybacks creating scarcity within this horizon.
  • What could change it: governance, more than market growth: all fees to buybacks 15.2%, issuance cut to a quarter 15.9%, both 34.8%; the best cell of the lever table is 49.1%, so none reaches even odds.
  • What would prove this wrong: annualised NEAR Intents fees above $439.2M at today's price and issuance. history.csv records the odds at every daily snapshot.
  • Locked in advance: the forecasts are written on NEAR (contract brayan-posterior.testnet), cannot be edited, and resolve on 2028-11-11; one command rebuilds every number from the committed data.

Written on NEAR, before the outcome

The registered forecasts, the scoreboard and the milestones are stored in the contract brayan-posterior.testnet (testnet). No method can edit or delete them. Since this transaction the account holds no full-access key: its only key can call resolve, so nobody, the author included, can replace the code or the records.

The contract refuses a resolution before its date. Once the date has passed, any NEAR account can write its own reading of the outcome next to the author's (attest): the author's word is one line among others.

  • 12 months: P = 0.054, resolves 2027-11-11 - transaction
  • 24 months: P = 0.053, resolves 2028-11-11 - transaction
  • 36 months: P = 0.061, resolves 2029-11-11 - transaction
  • scoreboard - transaction
  • milestones - transaction

The chain is also a data source: NEAR total supply is read from block headers on an archival RPC node. Between 2025-11-11 and 2026-11-11 that gives a net issuance of 31.77 million NEAR (2.48% a year), 0.00% away from the NearBlocks figure used in the model.

Read from the chain alone, the buyback wallet buybacks.multisignature.near gained 1.51 million NEAR between the end of 2026-08-13 and the end of 2026-11-11 (blocks 211225999 and 224135609) while total supply grew 7.95 million NEAR: a coverage of 19.0%, against 18.2% for the model's C_90 on the same date. Limits: it is the net inflow of NEAR into the wallet, so outflows or staking through a pool would distort it; it counts NEAR, while C_90 weighs each day in dollars; and a buyback is not a burn, the NEAR bought stays in a wallet.

Check: uv run python -m near_posterior.onchain, or run the same checks in your browser.

Under the hood

1. DataFees of 797 DeFi protocols and of NEAR Intents (DefiLlama); NEAR supply and price (NearBlocks), checked on the NEAR RPC.
2. FrozenRules written in PREREG.md before the download; every file pinned by sha256.
3. ModelKeep the 75 protocols that once had NEAR Intents' size and age; count how many grew fees at least k* in 24 months.
4. On NEARThe forecast goes into a NEAR contract that cannot edit or delete it.
5. ResolutionOn 2028-11-11 the outcome is written next to the forecast and scored.

The core of the method, simplified (near_posterior.forecast.b0):

k_star = issuance_in_dollars / buybacks        # growth the fees need
peers  = protocols at NEAR Intents' size and age, at any past month
P      = mean over peers of share(fee growth in 24 months >= k_star)
band   = bootstrap over peers (90 percent)
# replayed on past forecasts: beats trend extrapolation, still too optimistic

A thesis that can be proven wrong, in public

Every number here rebuilds from one command, and the forecast sits on NEAR until 2028-11-11. If token theses were published this way, investors could check track records instead of promises, and this one will be checked too.

What this cannot tell you

  • No price forecast. Price enters only as a scenario factor G_P (0.5, 1, 2).
  • Frequencies are not mechanisms. A base rate says how often fee growth of this size happened to comparable protocols, not why, and not whether NEAR Intents is like them.
  • DefiLlama lists what it lists. Protocols it does not track, or tracks late, are absent; the panel is what survived to be listed.
  • "Fees" differ across protocols: each DefiLlama adapter defines its own fees.
  • Capture and issuance are governance decisions. G_kappa and G_I are scenarios, not forecasts.
  • Aggregator data, not an on-chain rebuild: buybacks are DefiLlama holders revenue, supply and price are NearBlocks charts.
  • Age is the age in DefiLlama's data (days since the first day with fees > 0 in the panel), not the age since launch.
  • Dead fill uses the panel end D: an entity silent for more than 30 days before D is filled with zeros, so a backtest pool outcome can use knowledge of D rather than of its tau.
  • Class R selects protocols at a high point of their fees (scale band around NEAR Intents' current fees), so part of the typical decline in the class is regression to the mean.
  • Few independent market cycles: the class R origins at h = 24 run from 2020 to 2024, and one market regime can dominate the class.
  • Class R mixes fee types (trading fees, interest, staking yield). Restricted to trading-type categories (R_trading, 38 entities) the primary probability is 0.050 (0.008 to 0.100), against 0.053 for R.
